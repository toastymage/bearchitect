# Gemini Advice for becoming Strategist

My initial assessment is that while you've done a commendable job delegating tactical work to your architects, you're currently operating at a **senior architect/lead architect level**, but you're not yet consistently operating as an **architecture strategist**. The good news is that you have a solid foundation and clear areas for growth.

Let's break down my observations and then develop actionable routines.

**My Key Observations (Critical Mentor Mode On):**

1.  **Time Allocation Discrepancy:**
    * **Low Strategic Engagement:** Your "once a quarter" meeting with business leadership and "zero hours" on company strategy/market trends are glaring red flags. This is the core issue preventing you from being a strategist. You can't strategically guide architecture if you're not deeply immersed in the business's strategic direction.
    * **Reactive Administrative Load:** "Reactive - try to do them real time" for admin tasks and "always keep zero inbox" suggests you're constantly in a reactive mode, not proactive. This consumes mental bandwidth that should be used for strategic thought.
    * **Uninterrupted Time is Limited & Misplaced:** 2-3 hours of uninterrupted work is good, but if it's mostly "reviewing solutions and providing guidance," it's still focused on *your team's output*, not *your strategic input*. Your peak focus time (3-5 PM) is currently being underutilized for strategic deep dives.

2.  **Strategic Information Vacuum:**
    * "None. I am not aware of the company strategy." This is a significant gap. You cannot architect for the future if you don't know where the business is trying to go. It's like building a bridge without knowing the destination.
    * "Not invited to their offsites or strategy planning sessions." This reinforces the information vacuum and indicates a lack of perceived strategic value from the business side, which we need to change.

3.  **Stakeholder Engagement Gap:**
    * "Once a quarter in 1:1 setting" with critical non-IT business leaders is insufficient for truly understanding their needs and influencing their strategy. You're getting updates, not shaping direction.
    * "I expect culture to be open and inviting. I don't like to push." This is a critical mindset shift required for strategic influence. Strategists don't wait to be invited; they *earn* their seat at the table by demonstrating value and actively engaging. Sometimes, you *have* to push (with value, not just demands).

4.  **Team Empowerment - The Next Level:**
    * You've done well in delegating *ownership* to product architects. That's a great start.
    * However, "Generally they review with me to gain confidence" and the need for coaching in "Business domain, Integration architecture, Systems thinking and proactively understand architecture needs" indicates they are not yet fully empowered to make *strategic decisions* within their domain without your validation. This is holding you back from truly elevating.

**Conclusion:** You're stuck in a highly effective "review and guide" mode, which is valuable but prevents you from truly leading the *architectural strategy*. You're fixing the current ship well, but not charting the course for the fleet.

---

### Actionable Routines to Become an Architecture Strategist:

Here's a structured approach to integrate strategic activities into your week, leveraging your strengths and addressing the gaps.

**I. Strategic Time Blocking (Non-Negotiable)**

* **Rationale:** You need dedicated, uninterrupted time for high-level thinking, free from daily reactive tasks.
* **Routine:**
    * **"Strategic Horizon Scan" Block (1.5 hours, Bi-Weekly):**
        * **When:** Leverage your 3-5 PM peak productivity window. Schedule this for **Tuesday or Thursday afternoons**.
        * **Activity:** This is your dedicated time to consume strategic information.
            * Review internal company strategy documents (demand access if you don't have it).
            * Read industry reports, analyst insights (even free summaries), competitive analysis.
            * Listen to relevant podcasts or watch webinars on market trends in your domain (e.g., FinTech, Data Services).
            * Synthesize this information. What are the 3 biggest trends? How do they impact *our* business? What are the architectural implications?
    * **"Architectural Visioning & Roadmapping" Block (1.5 hours, Bi-Weekly, alternating with Horizon Scan):**
        * **When:** The other **Tuesday or Thursday afternoon** peak slot.
        * **Activity:** Based on your horizon scanning and business insights:
            * Begin sketching out 1-3 year architectural themes or capabilities.
            * Identify potential cross-product platform opportunities.
            * Consider technical debt in the context of *future* strategic needs, not just current pain.
            * Draft proposals or discussion points for future strategic discussions.
            * Review and refine existing strategic documents you've drafted (like your recent modernization presentation).

**II. Proactive Stakeholder Engagement Routines**

* **Rationale:** You need to bridge the gap with business leaders and gain a seat at their strategic table.
* **Routine:**
    * **"Strategic Business Pulse" 1:1s (30 mins, Monthly):**
        * **Who:** Head of Fund Admin, Head of TA Operations, Head of Performance & Attribution, Head of Market Data Services. Pick **one** each week or two, rotating through them.
        * **Activity:**
            * **Prepare:** Before each meeting, research their current business initiatives, recent performance, and industry news related to their domain. Come with *informed questions*.
            * **Questions to Ask:** "What are your top 3 strategic priorities for the next 6-12 months?" "What are the biggest challenges you foresee in achieving those priorities?" "Are there any emerging market opportunities you're excited about?" "How do you see technology *enabling* or *limiting* your ability to achieve X goal?"
            * **Your Value Proposition:** Don't just listen. Briefly offer how architecture *could* potentially support their goals or mitigate risks. "Based on what you just said, our proposed [TACC modernization/new data platform] could significantly accelerate [their strategic goal]. Can we explore that further?"
            * **Goal:** Move beyond "updates" to "strategic partnership." Get on their radar as someone who understands their business and can help them win.
    * **"Informal Insights Gathering" (Ad-hoc, Daily/Weekly):**
        * **Activity:** Be opportunistic. If you see a key business leader in the hallway, at lunch, or on a break, engage them briefly. Ask a quick, open-ended question about their day, a recent challenge, or a market trend. These informal moments build rapport and provide valuable nuggets of information.

**III. Elevating Team Empowerment Routines**

* **Rationale:** Freeing up your mental bandwidth requires your architects to truly own their domains, including strategic thinking.
* **Routine:**
    * **"Architect's Strategic Deep Dive" (30-45 mins, Bi-weekly, during 1:1s or separate):**
        * **Who:** Focus on the architects who are ready for more ownership.
        * **Activity:** Beyond their current solution review:
            * **Business Context Challenge:** "For Product X, what are the next 3 major business capabilities we expect in the next 18 months, and how does your current architecture prepare for those?"
            * **Future Proofing:** "Looking at the industry trends we discussed, how might this product's architecture need to evolve in 2 years to stay competitive?"
            * **Integration Strategy:** "How does your product's architecture interact with Product Y and Product Z, and what are the strategic integration points or potential bottlenecks we need to address?" (Focus on the "how" of cross-domain integration, which you identified as a gap).
            * **Problem Identification, Not Just Solutioning:** Challenge them to identify potential architectural problems *before* they become emergencies.
    * **"Systems Thinking Workshop" (1 hour, Monthly Team Meeting slot):**
        * **Activity:** Instead of just architecture reviews, dedicate a portion of your team meeting to a strategic architectural discussion.
            * Pick a complex cross-cutting concern (e.g., "Our approach to real-time data streaming," "How we handle security authentication across all products," "The future of our AI/ML infrastructure").
            * Have architects present their current solutions/challenges in that area, but then facilitate a discussion on the *strategic implications* and *enterprise-wide opportunities or risks*. Encourage them to think beyond their product.
            * **Goal:** Build their "systems thinking" muscle and foster proactive architectural awareness across the team.

**IV. Optimizing Communication & Reactive Load**

* **Rationale:** Your current "zero inbox" and reactive admin approach is efficient but drains strategic capacity.
* **Routine:**
    * **"Inbox & Message Batching" (30 mins, 2x daily):**
        * **When:** Schedule specific times (e.g., 9 AM and 4 PM) to process emails and messages. Outside these times, close your email/IM client or turn off notifications during strategic blocks.
        * **Activity:** Respond, delegate, or archive. This frees your mind from constant reactivity. Your architects are independent; they don't need instant replies unless it's a critical production issue.
    * **"Strategic Output Review" (30 mins, End of Week):**
        * **Activity:** Review your strategic notes from the week.
            * What key insights did you gain from business leaders or horizon scanning?
            * What's the one strategic action you need to take next week? (e.g., follow up with Head of Fund Admin on X, draft a 1-pager on Y architectural initiative).
            * This keeps you accountable to your strategic shift.

**V. Leveraging Your Boss**

* **Routine:**
    * **"Strategic Alignment Update" (10-15 mins, Bi-weekly during your 1:1):**
        * **Activity:** Use a portion of your weekly 1:1 to specifically discuss strategic architectural topics.
            * "Based on my recent conversations with [Business Leader], I'm seeing X emerging business need that our current architecture needs to prepare for. I'm starting to explore Y architectural initiative to address it. Your thoughts?"
            * "I've been reviewing Z industry trend, and it suggests we should consider [technical shift]. I'm looking for opportunities to get invited to more strategic business planning sessions to ensure our architecture is fully aligned."
            * **Goal:** Keep him informed of your strategic efforts, gain his high-level guidance, and potentially enlist his help in opening doors to strategic business meetings.

**Implementation Plan (Start Small, Scale Up):**

1.  **Commit to the Strategic Blocks:** Immediately put those "Strategic Horizon Scan" and "Architectural Visioning" blocks on your calendar. Treat them as sacred.
2.  **Pick One Business Leader:** Start with monthly 1:1s with just *one* of the identified business leaders you interact with least. Master that, then add another.
3.  **Integrate Strategic Team Discussions:** Incorporate the "Architect's Strategic Deep Dive" questions into your existing 1:1s or dedicate a small portion of your team meeting to the "Systems Thinking Workshop."
4.  **Experiment with Batching:** Try the email/IM batching for a week and see how it impacts your focus.

This is a marathon, not a sprint. You're shifting deeply ingrained habits. Don't get discouraged if you miss a block or get pulled into something tactical. The key is consistent effort and continuously protecting your strategic time and mental space.

Your biggest challenge will be the mindset shift from "I wait to be invited" to "I demonstrate value and earn my seat." Start delivering strategic insights, even small ones, and watch how the invitations start to follow.
